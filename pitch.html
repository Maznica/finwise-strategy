Strategy · M1

The growth bet

Hypothesis

The core belief

FinWise’s biggest growth problem is that the product creates value for exactly one person, so nothing built during the reverse trial becomes something anyone would pay to keep or lose by leaving., because over 13 months deep feature adoption rose 5.4× (Modeling 10.78% → 58.26%) while trial→paid stayed locked in a 0.21-point band and churn outran new customers in all 13 months. Usage went up; neither conversion nor retention responded. The missing ingredient isn’t engagement — it’s dependency..

_Working notes: I think FinWise biggest problem is that only 2 % of customers who begin a trial convert to paying customers.
As the majority of the marketing budget is going to paid acquissition to drive trial sign-ups and only 2 % of them becoming paid customers. It is really problematic.
So, I think that they are not able to show the value in the trial period and therefore, only 2 % convert to paid users.
In addition to that also the retention is not that good as only 40 % stay with finwise after one year. That means for me that even one year some of the customers are not able to recognize the value.
So the problem is that the vlaue is hard to realize for the customers. Therefore the reverse trial also not work because it seams that the downgrade doesn't hurt. Evidence: - Most of the marketing budget goes to paid acquisition to drive trial sign-ups
- But only 2 % of trial customers become paid customers: This means that majority of the users never recognize the value and therefore never got far enough in to feel the loss by reverse trial.
- That means, adding more money to acquisition is no longer driving meaningful growth. The acquisition isn't the constraint.

Moreover, 60 % churn means that the failure continues after payment.

Against the data: The data at one hand confirmed and at another challenged my hypotheses:
Confirmed:

I claimed users never reach real value. Import Data at 39.37% backs that — the majority of trials never load their own numbers.
I claimed the churn problem is worse than the headline. It is: churn exceeds new customers in all 13 months, 317 vs 128, net −189. The scenario said “60% churn”; the data says the customer base is shrinking every single month.
I ranked acquisition last. The data is harsher than I was — not diminishing returns, no returns (r = 0.09).

Challenged — and this is the real hit:

My whole hypothesis rested on “get more users to value and conversion follows.” The data refuses that. Modeling adoption went 10.78% → 58.26%, a 5.4× rise in deep feature usage, and trial→paid went 2.05% → 2.06%.

Users got substantially more engaged and it bought FinWise nothing. My predicted mechanism — activation improves, conversion follows — already ran as a natural experiment over 13 months, and it failed._

Activation · M2

The Aha moment

Aha moment

When value lands

Actually there are kind of two Aha moments. One like prerequisity and real one. The prerequisity is that users connect their bank and see nice chart with their real data. But we saw in module 1 that although this is important it is not real aha moment. Althought engagement was increasing the trial to paid conversion wasn't. Nevertheless it will be still helpful to get as much user as possible to this step as it is a prerequisite to the real aha moment.

The real aha moment is:

A second person — the owner's accountant or bookkeeper — looks at the owner's
real numbers inside FinWise and comments on them.

Reason: a single-user account has nothing to lose at the day-15 downgrade. A shared
account has a second person depending on it. Every screen must move toward getting
that second person into the account.

That’s the difference: seeing a chart is information. Having your accountant in there is “my books live here now.”
One thing to be clear about: the chart still matters — but as the step before, not the Aha itself. Because you can’t ask your accountant to look at an empty account. It’s like inviting a friend over to see your treehouse before you’ve built the treehouse.

Retention · M3

The habit mechanic

Mechanic

The habit loop

Module 3 · Retention & Engagement. The engagement mechanic that builds the habit loop, with rationale and a wireframe.

I have chosen a some new mechanic - "Whose Turn" with combination of progress bar as none of the free standard mechanics fit cleanly.
Whose Turn is a persistent, repairable, two-sided state: Waiting on you · Waiting on Jana (accountant), each item visibly ageing. It taps loss aversion, but socially anchored — the loss isn’t a number resetting, it’s being the person holding things up.

The churn diagnosis it answers. Module 1’s data killed the obvious mechanism: deep feature adoption rose 5.4× (Modeling 10.78% → 58.26%) while trial→paid moved 0.01 points, and churn outran new customers in all 13 months (317 vs 128). Engagement went up and bought nothing. The missing ingredient was never engagement — it was dependency. Any mechanic that a solo owner can complete alone reproduces that failure exactly, which is why the progress bar lost: it can hit 100% without the accountant.

Whose Turn cannot be cleared alone. There are no items without a second person. The metric it moves is dependency, not activity.

Leading

Predicts outcomes

- **Share of people started the imported data (connection to the bank):** It is the hard gate on the North Star — no user can reach a modeling output without passing it, so every point gained here is a point of North Star volume, and it responds within days of any onboarding change.
- **Import - first modeling output rate with good data (%modelling rate):** It is the second multiplicative term in the North Star — of those who do import, how many actually reach a good output. Unfortunately in my prototype leading 1 and 2 are collapsed as the model is generated automatically.

Experiment · M5

How I test it

Method

Test design

**Method:** A/B Test**Why:** There are several reasons:1. Precision is what this bet needs, and therefore an A/B test is the method which gives it. This is a structural bet — M1 explicitly chose the loop over the faster funnel fix, accepting "if I'm wrong, I'm wrong slowly and expensively."2. The randomisation unit is available and not contaminated.3. Holdout test would be also nice but more for retention ( churn) metric.
Hypothesis

Metric + success

If we make inviting a second person the primary call-to-action of the reverse trial — surfaced at first login and again the moment a user generates their first cash-flow report, with the invitee receiving a permanent free view-and-comment seat — then the share of trial accounts reaching 2+ active users by day 14 will rise from baseline to ≥25%, and trial→paid among multi-user accounts will run at ≥2× single-user accounts (~4% vs ~2%), because a single-user account has almost nothing to lose at the day-15 downgrade while a shared account contains a working relationship the downgrade can remove

**Primary metric:** % of trial accounts with 2+ active users by day 14. "Active" = the invitee has signed in and either viewed a report or left a comment (per M2, commented is the stronger signal; viewed is the minimum bar). Measured intent-to-treat on all accounts randomised at signup., target: Primary ≥ 25% of trial accounts multi-user by day 14, at p < 0.05. Anything below that is a Modest Win → iterate, don't ship.

_Predicted outcome: Treatment reaches 25–30% of trial accounts multi-user by day 14, against a control baseline in the low single digits. Trial→paid among multi-user accounts runs at roughly 4% vs ~2%. If successful: 1. Ship to 100% behind a feature flag, rolled out incrementally.
2. Open the committed 5% long-term holdout to test the claim this bet actually rests on — that dependency fixes the 2.48:1 churn ratio.
3. Iterate on the second half of the loop: invite acceptance and invitee activation
4. Feed the multi-user accounts into the M3 "Whose Turn" mechanic — it has no fuel without them.. If unsuccessful: Diagnose what was going on with additional metrics.
1. Low invite send rate - iterate on cta to invite
2. High send rate, low acceptance - accountant doesn't see a reason to come, iterate on invitation
3. High acceptance, no conversion lift - maybe our assumption about dependency was not correct, stop and try another test.._

Recommendation

The move

Restructure the reverse trial around a second person instead of around features. Every new trial account gets a permanent, free view-and-comment seat it can give to its bookkeeper or accountant, surfaced as the primary call-to-action at first login and again the moment the owner’s first cash-flow report renders. At day 15 the account still downgrades — but a shared account downgrades out of a working relationship (comments, a half-finished reconciliation, someone else’s time already spent), not out of a feature list. The paid line moves from “which features the owner can see” to “what the owner and their accountant need to keep working in here.”

**The pricing bet:** Packaging matched to value:The module’s test for this bet is whether the free/paid line sits where users feel they need more, rather than where the business’s conversion goal wants it. FinWise’s line currently sits where the business put it — around premium features
Model signal

What success looks like

It targets my primary M4 leading indicator directly: % of trial accounts with 2+ active users by day 14 (invite accepted and the second person viewed or commented — not invites sent). This is the only indicator on my M4 list that tracks the mechanism the recommendation actually rests on.
