Why a question earns its place here: it changes the flow and returns, in week one, the number the whole bet depends on — the share of trial users who work with someone. One tap for that is cheap.

Screen 2 of 4

Get real financial data in. The narrowest point in the whole product.
Action

Cut: "explore with sample data". It fakes the value and guarantees an empty account on day 15 — the exact failure FinWise is already in. Only 39.37% of trials cross this line today.

Screen 4 of 4

Invite your accountant

Job
Get the second person in. This is the Aha trigger.
Action
One email field, one send. Subject and message already written.

Why here and not at first login: you don't ask an accountant to review an empty account. The invite only makes sense once there's a report to look at — so screen 1 plants it and screen 3 asks.

The Aha moment

A second person touched the numbers

Not the chart. The comment. A single-user account has nothing to lose when the reverse trial downgrades on day 15 — a shared one has someone depending on it.

The obvious candidate was ruled out by the data: deep feature adoption rose 5.4× over 13 months (10.78% → 58.26%) while trial→paid moved 2.05% → 2.06%. Users reached value alone, and it converted nobody.

Connect bank→ First real report→ Second person comments
Branch · sole trader

The collapse condition, surfaced on day one

If a large share of trials answer "just me", the collaboration loop has no fuel and the bet fails — not because the flow is wrong, but because the premise is.

Worth finding out with one tap on screen 1 rather than after a quarter of building. The branch stays deliberately thin.

The definition

FinWise's Aha moment

A trial account reaches Aha when a second invited user has viewed or commented on a report built from the owner's own connected financial data, within 7 days of sign-up.

Seven days, not fourteen — the reverse trial downgrades on day 15, so the shared account needs a week of real use before anything is taken away.

Still a candidate, not a confirmed Aha. Confirm it by splitting retained-past-90-day accounts by active users per account. Flat retention across account size kills it.
