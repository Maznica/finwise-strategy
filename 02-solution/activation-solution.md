# The Solution · FinWise

> Module 2 · Acquisition & Activation. The onboarding solution and the Aha moment that makes value land.

## Aha moment

Actually there are kind of two Aha moments. One like prerequisity and real one. The prerequisity is that users connect their bank and see nice chart with their real data. But we saw in module 1 that although this is important it is not real aha moment. Althought engagement was increasing the trial to paid conversion wasn't. Nevertheless it will be still helpful to get as much user as possible to this step as it is a prerequisite to the real aha moment.

The real aha moment is:

  A second person — the owner's accountant or bookkeeper — looks at the owner's
  real numbers inside FinWise and comments on them.

Reason: a single-user account has nothing to lose at the day-15 downgrade. A shared
account has a second person depending on it. Every screen must move toward getting
that second person into the account.

That’s the difference: seeing a chart is information. Having your accountant in there is “my books live here now.”
One thing to be clear about: the chart still matters — but as the step before, not the Aha itself. Because you can’t ask your accountant to look at an empty account. It’s like inviting a friend over to see your treehouse before you’ve built the treehouse.

## Why this activates

Two different claims are doing work here, and they need separate arguments.

Claim 1 — why the flow raises activation

Four things change, in order:

① Nothing stands in front of the gate.
Today only 39.37% of trials ever connect data. The flow puts connecting at screen 2 — after one tap, before any tour, any profile fields, any pricing. 

② The report is delivered, not assembled.
Most products hand you a configured-nothing dashboard and call it “value.” Screen 3 arrives already built: range picked, transactions categorised, currency and business name inferred. The user’s effort to reach first value drops to zero. That collapses time-to-Aha-0 from “whenever they get round to setting it up” to one session — and time-to-Aha is the metric with the most leverage, because every extra minute is another chance to lose interest.

③ The report creates a reason to share that serves the user, not FinWise.
The September flag — £15,220 in, £16,980 out — isn’t decoration. Without something worth a second opinion, “invite your accountant” is a favour you’re asking the owner to do for you. With it, sharing is the owner’s own next move: they have a question and someone who answers questions like that for a living.

④ The invite is native, not bolted on.
This is Module 2’s test for a real loop. A referral code is FinWise’s business inserted into the user’s life. “Send your books to the person who checks your books” is the user’s life. The product isn’t asking for marketing help; it’s doing the job the owner signed up to do.

So activation goes up because friction before the gate drops, effort at the gate drops, and a new activation event exists that previously lived in a settings menu nobody opened.

Claim 2 — why it converts, which is the harder argument

Here’s the trap. The Module 1 data refuted “more value → more conversion.” Modeling adoption rose 5.4×; conversion moved 0.01 points. So raising activation, on its own, is not an argument that conversion follows. You cannot reuse that mechanism.

The conversion argument is a different one: the second person changes what the day-15 decision actually is. Three things happen at once.

① Loss aversion gets an object.
A reverse trial converts on the downgrade hurting. Today the account on day 15 is one person looking at their own data — take the premium features away and nothing is lost, because nothing was built. A shared account on day 15 contains a working relationship: comments, a reconciliation half-done, someone else’s time already spent. Now there is something the downgrade can remove.

② The decision stops being abstract and stops being solo.
Today the owner is asked, alone, “is this worth paying for?” — a hypothetical about future value, which is the hardest question in software to answer yes to. With a second person in the account, the question becomes “do I want to break the thing my accountant and I are working in, and tell her we’re moving?” 

③ A seat is a mechanical billing trigger.
Persuasion is a bad conversion engine; a second user is an arithmetic one. “You now have two people in this account” is a reason to pay that doesn’t require anyone to be convinced of anything. It turns the 2% ceiling from a persuasion problem into a volume question — how many accounts get to two users.

And retention follows from the same thing, for free: churning now requires two people to stop caring, not one. That’s why the Module 1 doc bets collaboration fixes the 2.48:1 churn ratio as a byproduct.


Where the logic breaks — three honest gaps

① The owner has nobody to invite. Then the loop has no fuel and the flow can’t reach Aha at all. Then we stayed just with the first aha moment which might not be the real one.

② The second person accepts but never engages. Then you have a seat, not a dependency. This is why the Aha definition says viewed or commented, and why commented is the stronger signal.
