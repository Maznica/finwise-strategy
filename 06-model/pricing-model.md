# The Model · FinWise

> Module 6 · Pricing & Monetization. The pricing & packaging recommendation memo for FinWise.

## Stage + model

**Stage:** Stage 1 · Value Creation, I think Finwise is still in value creation phase because:
1. Only 39.37% of trials ever import their own financial data. Three in five trial users never load a single number, so they never reach even the prerequisite for value
2. rial→paid sat at ~1.99% for all 13 months, inside a 0.21-point band (2.05% → 2.06%), while deep-feature adoption rose 5.4× (Modeling 10.78% → 58.26%).

Supporting: Churn exceeded new paid customers in all 13 months - therefore not revenue expansion

**Model:** Freemium, FinWise already runs freemium: a 15-day reverse trial that downgrades to a usable free tier. The model is not the problem. Where the line sits is the problem.
The move: make a permanent free view-and-comment seat for the owner’s accountant or bookkeeper

Key trade-off:
Subscription with a seat-based unit is the obvious alternative  — charge per person, and the second user becomes a mechanical billing trigger rather than a persuasion problem. I’m passing on it for one reason: charging for the second seat taxes the exact behaviour FinWise needs to manufacture. At 39.37% import and 2% conversion, FinWise has no shortage of revenue per account — it has a shortage of accounts containing anything worth paying for. 

What breaks if I’m wrong: if accountants come in free and the owner still never needs premium to work with them, I’ve widened the free tier, raised costs, and moved nothing. That’s the cannibalisation risk, and it’s real — which is why the paid line has to sit on the shared workflow, not on access.

_____

## Recommendation

Restructure the reverse trial around a second person instead of around features. Every new trial account gets a permanent, free view-and-comment seat it can give to its bookkeeper or accountant, surfaced as the primary call-to-action at first login and again the moment the owner’s first cash-flow report renders. At day 15 the account still downgrades — but a shared account downgrades out of a working relationship (comments, a half-finished reconciliation, someone else’s time already spent), not out of a feature list. The paid line moves from “which features the owner can see” to “what the owner and their accountant need to keep working in here.”

**The pricing bet:** Packaging matched to value:The module’s test for this bet is whether the free/paid line sits where users feel they need more, rather than where the business’s conversion goal wants it. FinWise’s line currently sits where the business put it — around premium features

_____

## Model signal

It targets my primary M4 leading indicator directly: % of trial accounts with 2+ active users by day 14 (invite accepted and the second person viewed or commented — not invites sent). This is the only indicator on my M4 list that tracks the mechanism the recommendation actually rests on.
